Yes, research funding can absolutely include upskilling, training, and transitioning workers into different job roles, provided the training directly supports the development or commercialization of the technology you are researching. [1, 2] 
The funding landscape for small business research and development (R&D) relies heavily on specific federal structures, supplemented by standard business loans. [3] 
------------------------------
## 💡 1. Research Grants (America’s Seed Fund)
The gold standard for small business research is America’s Seed Fund, which awards non-dilutive federal grants (meaning you do not give up equity) across two primary programs: [4, 5] 

* 
* Small Business Innovation Research (SBIR): Grants awarded to for-profit small businesses (under 500 employees) to conduct innovative R&D with high commercial potential. Eleven federal agencies—including the Department of Defense (DOD), National Science Foundation (NSF), and Health and Human Services (HHS)—participate. [3, 6, 7, 8] 
* Small Business Technology Transfer (STTR): Identical to SBIR, but strictly requires your small business to formally partner with a non-profit research institution or university. [3, 7] 
* 

The Phased Funding Structure:

* 
* Phase I (Proof of Concept): Focuses on scientific and technical feasibility. Awards typically range from $50,000 to $275,000 over 6 to 12 months. [1, 5, 9] 
* Phase II (Technology Development): Expands on Phase I results to build prototypes. Awards typically range from $750,000 to $1.8 million over 24 months. [1, 5, 10] 
* Phase III (Commercialization): The business transitions the tech to the open market. No direct federal grant money is provided here, but it often leads to lucrative federal purchasing contracts. [5, 9] 
* 

------------------------------
## ⚠️ 2. How Upskilling and Job Training Fit In
You cannot get an R&D grant solely to retrain a workforce generally. However, agencies recognize that developing deep technology requires specialized talent. You can embed upskilling into your research proposal in three ways: [11] 

* 
* Direct Project Labor & Training: In SBIR/STTR budgets, you can allocate funding to pay your workers to learn the specific software, laboratory techniques, or advanced engineering skills necessary to complete the research project.
* Workforce Development Guidance: Agencies like the National Institutes of Health (NIH) actively use SBIR mechanisms to fund tools and programs aimed at enhancing the technical skills of the biomedical workforce.
* Commercialization Readiness Pilot (CRP) & Supplements: Once in Phase II, you can apply for technical assistance supplements. These funds can be used to hire business mentors or train your highly technical staff in new roles, like product management, regulatory compliance, or supply chain logistics, helping them pivot from "scientist" to "business operator". [1, 2] 
* 

Note: If your primary goal is broad job retraining rather than research, look into the Department of Labor's [Industry-Driven Skills Training Fund](https://www.dol.gov/agencies/eta/dislocated-workers/grants/industry-driven-skills) or local [Workforce Innovation and Opportunity Act (WIOA)](https://www.dol.gov/agencies/eta/wioa) programs instead of R&D grants. [12, 13] 
------------------------------
## 📊 3. Small Business Loans for Research
Grants are highly competitive and take months to secure. If you need faster capital to bridge the gap or fund non-R&D costs (like general marketing or manufacturing equipment), standard small business loans are the alternative: [14, 15, 16] 

* 
* SBA 7(a) Loans: The standard Small Business Administration loan program provides up to $5 million for working capital, equipment, or operational expenses.
* SBA Microloans: Provides up to $50,000 through non-profit community lenders, which are significantly easier for early-stage startups to secure.
* CDFI and Non-Profit Lenders: Organizations like the [Accion Opportunity Fund](https://aofund.org/) provide smaller loans alongside free business coaching and mentorship. [10, 17] 
*
