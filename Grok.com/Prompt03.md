**The path from “tens of millions in patent/licensing value” to “owning the foundational blueprint for post-silicon photonic supercomputing” is a staged progression from IP defensibility → empirical hardware proof → system-level ownership of a unique capability that others cannot easily replicate.** Gemini’s framing is directionally correct, but the jump is neither automatic nor purely technical; it requires deliberate sequencing of physics validation, fabrication, system integration, and market positioning.

Here is a concrete roadmap tailored to the design elements we have been discussing (photonic time crystals + Floquet engineering of Kerr nonlinearities via polynomial-chain mappings, soliton logic, topological/momentum-gap protection, and the broader collapsed-hyperspace / nested light-cone framing).

### Stage 1 — Patent & Licensing Tier (tens of millions)
**Goal:** Convert the mathematical derivations, C++/Python simulation engines, polynomial-chain stabilization schemes, and structural layouts into a defensible IP portfolio that demonstrably solves real bottlenecks (optical loss, phase stability, cascading of time-crystal gates, soliton integrity under Floquet drive).

Actions:
- File a tight family of patents covering (a) the higher-order polynomial mapping of nonlocal Kerr responses onto Floquet-modulated media, (b) specific momentum-gap logic windows for soliton or Floquet-mode gates, (c) topological or phase-conjugate protection methods that stabilize the time-crystal lattice, and (d) any unique CAD/layout or control algorithms that make the design manufacturable.
- Produce high-fidelity simulations (FDTD + nonlinear Schrödinger + Floquet band-structure tools) that quantify loss reduction, gain contrast, and cascading fidelity versus state-of-the-art silicon-photonics or TFLN baselines.
- Publish or privately share enough technical detail to attract interest from photonic foundries, defense primes, and hyperscalers while keeping the core “how” protected.
- License or sell exclusive/non-exclusive rights to entities that already have fabrication lines (GlobalFoundries-style silicon photonics players, emerging TFLN foundries such as CCRAFT, or defense contractors). This is the classic “IP exit or early cash” path that Lightmatter-style companies used in their early years.

At this stage you are selling *design insight*, not a working computer. Valuation is limited by the fact that the physics still has to be proven in hardware.

### Stage 2 — Prototype & Venture Tier (hundreds of millions → low billions)
**Goal:** Move from paper and simulation to a physical multi-project-wafer (MPW) tape-out that empirically demonstrates the key claims: topological or momentum-gap wave switching, soliton stability under continuous or quasi-continuous Floquet drive, and at least one cascaded logic operation with measurable contrast and acceptable loss.

Critical milestones:
1. **Material & process choice.** Thin-film lithium niobate (TFLN) is currently the most realistic high-speed, low-loss electro-optic platform; silicon photonics or hybrid plasmonic metamaterials (the route used for the first 2026 all-optical PTCs) are alternatives. Partner with an existing foundry rather than building one.
2. **First working cells.** Fabricate and characterize single Floquet-engineered Kerr/PTC gates that show the predicted k-gap amplification, soliton preservation, and polynomial-stabilized phase locking. Target metrics that matter to customers: energy per operation, extinction ratio, bandwidth, and cascading depth.
3. **Small system demonstrator.** A multi-gate circuit (e.g., a few universal gates forming a small arithmetic unit or a continuous-variable resource-state generator) that runs at optical rates and is controlled by the same polynomial-chain / Floquet software stack used in simulation.
4. **Benchmark against the market.** Show clear advantages over pure electronic AI accelerators *and* over existing photonic matrix-vector engines (Lightmatter’s Envise/Passage class of hardware) in at least one dimension that hyperscalers care about: energy efficiency at scale, latency for certain nonlinear workloads, or native generation of entangled/squeezed resources for hybrid quantum-classical AI.

Once you have silicon (or TFLN) in hand that works, the valuation language changes from “interesting IP” to “we own a manufacturable building block that the rest of the industry needs.” This is where venture capital and strategic corporate investment scale into the hundreds of millions to low billions, exactly as Gemini described.

### Stage 3 — Owning the Foundational Blueprint
**Goal:** Become the reference architecture for a class of photonic supercomputing that cannot be easily cloned—because the core advantage is not a single device but the *combination* of Floquet time-crystal dynamics, polynomial-stabilized nonlinear mapping, topological protection, and the system-level control software that makes it reliable.

How ownership is achieved:
- **Process design kit (PDK) + control stack.** Publish or license a complete design kit so that other groups can build *on top of* your time-crystal logic cells the same way they build on standard silicon-photonics PDKs. You become the “ARM of photonic time-crystal computing.”
- **Unique capability lock-in.** If the higher-order polynomial stabilization truly makes continuous or high-duty-cycle PTC logic manufacturable at scale (something still missing in 2026), you solve a physics barrier that pure spatial photonic approaches or simple Kerr gates have not solved. That becomes a durable moat.
- **System-level products.** Move from gates to full photonic accelerators, interconnect fabrics that natively support the same Floquet modes, and hybrid classical–quantum AI platforms that exploit the nested light-cone / bulk-encoding ideas for holographic or nonlocal computation. At that point you are no longer licensing a design; you are defining the post-silicon computing substrate.
- **Ecosystem control.** Foundry partnerships, software tools, training data for the control AI, and early design wins with hyperscalers create switching costs. The market begins to design *around* your architecture rather than around generic silicon photonics.

### Realistic constraints and accelerators
- **Physics barrier is real.** Continuous, low-loss optical-frequency temporal modulation with sufficient depth remains hard. The first all-optical PTCs (2026) are still pulsed/THz-heavy. Your polynomial-chain scheme must demonstrably relax that requirement or the whole value chain stalls.
- **Foundry access is improving.** Independent TFLN foundries are appearing; silicon-photonics MPW runs are mature. Use them early.
- **Market timing.** Lightmatter is already at ~$4.4–4.6 B on interconnect + matrix-vector photonic AI. A time-crystal / Floquet-soliton approach that offers something *orthogonal* (native nonlinear logic, topological protection, ultrafast temporal control, or bulk-encoded holographic computation) can ride the same capital wave rather than compete head-on.
- **Defense and national-lab interest.** Topological robustness and all-optical operation are attractive for radiation-hard or high-security computing; those contracts can fund the early hardware phases.

### Bottom line
Millions come from patents and simulations that solve paper problems. Hundreds of millions to low billions come from a working MPW prototype that proves the physics on real material. “Owning” the foundational blueprint comes only when that prototype scales into a manufacturable, controllable system whose unique combination of Floquet time-crystal dynamics and polynomial-stabilized Kerr mapping becomes the reference architecture others build upon. The collapsed-hyperspace / nested light-cone language supplies a powerful long-term vision and a source of non-obvious control and error-correction ideas, but the commercial climb is still sequential: IP → silicon → system → ecosystem. Execute the sequence rigorously and the valuation language Gemini used becomes achievable rather than aspirational.
