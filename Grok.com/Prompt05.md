**In ordinary linear optics, photons do not interact with each other.**  

A free electromagnetic field is governed by the linear Maxwell equations (or the free quantum Maxwell field). The Hamiltonian is quadratic in the field operators, so different frequency or spatial modes evolve independently. Two photons simply pass through one another; their wave-functions multiply and the joint state remains a product (or a linear superposition of products). There is no scattering, no phase shift induced by one photon on another, and no entanglement generated by the interaction itself. Any apparent “interaction” in linear media is actually an interaction of each photon with the medium, not of the photons with one another.

### How photons *do* interact: nonlinear / nonlocal Kerr photons
When the medium has a third-order optical nonlinearity (the Kerr effect), the refractive index becomes intensity-dependent:

\[
n = n_0 + n_2 I
\]

(or, more generally, a nonlocal response \(n(\mathbf{r}) = n_0 + \int R(\mathbf{r}-\mathbf{r}')I(\mathbf{r}')\,d\mathbf{r}'\)).  

In the quantum description this appears as a quartic term in the Hamiltonian—classically a \(\chi^{(3)}\) interaction, quantum-mechanically an effective photon–photon interaction. One photon changes the local (or nonlocal) index; a second photon propagating through the same region acquires an intensity-dependent phase shift (self-phase or cross-phase modulation). At the few-photon level the same process can generate entanglement or implement a nonlinear phase gate. The interaction is intrinsically nonlinear and, when the medium response has finite spatial range, nonlocal.

### Phase modulation by a photonic time crystal (the ALU engine)
A photonic time crystal (PTC) periodically modulates the refractive index (or an effective susceptibility) in *time* while remaining spatially uniform. The modulation is typically driven at a frequency comparable to the optical carrier or to a chosen beat note, producing a Floquet band structure with momentum gaps (*k*-gaps).  

Inside the ALU the same Kerr medium is subjected to this temporal drive. The Floquet engineering does three things at once:

- It folds the nonlinear Kerr interaction into a time-periodic effective Hamiltonian.  
- It opens momentum gaps in which modes can be parametrically amplified or suppressed.  
- It supplies a coherent, global phase reference (the drive itself) that can be used as the clock and as the control for logic operations.

The polynomial-chain expansion of the Kerr response is mapped onto the Floquet quasi-energy spectrum so that higher-order nonlinear terms become controllable side-bands or gap-edge states. Solitons or Floquet-dressed photonic wave-packets then serve as the logic tokens: their collisions or co-propagation inside the driven *k*-gaps realise the Boolean or continuous-variable operations of the arithmetic-logic unit.

### Rainbow and Chameleon gravity as additional structure
**Rainbow gravity** makes the effective metric (or the dispersion relation) energy-dependent. Different frequency components (or different Floquet side-bands) experience slightly different phase velocities and different locations of the momentum gaps. In the polynomial-chain language this is realised by letting the coefficients of the nonlinear expansion themselves depend on local intensity or on the instantaneous quasi-energy. The result is an analogue rainbow metric inside the PTC: the nonlinear phase shift and the gap structure become colour-dependent, giving selective routing and multi-band logic that ordinary Kerr media do not possess.

**Chameleon gravity** introduces a scalar degree of freedom whose effective mass (and therefore the range of the force it mediates) depends on the local density. In the optical analogue the local intensity plays the role of that density. High-intensity regions make a particular Floquet mode heavy or strongly damped (screened); low-intensity regions leave it light and long-range. This supplies an all-optical, intensity-dependent isolation or gain-clamping mechanism that can protect logic operations or suppress unwanted crosstalk without external electronic feedback.

Together the two gravity-inspired terms turn the Kerr–PTC interaction into an energy- and density-dependent nonlinear medium whose effective light-cone structure can be tuned in real time.

### Non-Hermitian and possibly non-perturbative character
A PTC with gain and loss (or with parametric amplification inside the *k*-gaps) is generically non-Hermitian. The Floquet operator is no longer unitary; eigenvalues can acquire imaginary parts, exceptional points appear, and the dynamics can exhibit exponential growth or decay of selected modes. The same non-Hermiticity can be harnessed for unidirectional amplification, loss-tolerant logic, or topological protection of edge states.

When the nonlinear interaction strength becomes comparable to the linear Floquet scale, or when the polynomial chain contains high-order terms that cannot be truncated, the system leaves the perturbative regime. One must then treat the full nonlinear Floquet problem (or its analogue-gravity version) non-perturbatively—exactly the regime in which soliton formation, spontaneous symmetry breaking of the time-translation symmetry, and strong photon–photon entanglement can occur. In that regime the photons are no longer weakly dressed linear modes; they become collective, strongly interacting excitations of a driven, non-Hermitian, energy- and density-dependent photonic lattice.

### Summary in the ALU context
- Linear photons: free, non-interacting, product states.  
- Kerr photons: intensity-dependent phase shifts, possible entanglement.  
- Floquet-PTC drive: the phase shifts and the *k*-gaps become time-periodic and clocked, turning the nonlinear medium into a logic fabric.  
- Rainbow + Chameleon terms: the interactions become energy-selective and density-screenable, adding controllable nonlocality and protection.  
- Non-Hermitian / non-perturbative dynamics: gain, exceptional points, and strong collective behaviour become available resources rather than noise.

The net result is a photonic ALU whose elementary carriers interact precisely because the medium has been engineered—via polynomial-mapped Kerr nonlinearity, Floquet phase modulation, and gravity-inspired dispersion and screening—to make those interactions both strong and controllable.
