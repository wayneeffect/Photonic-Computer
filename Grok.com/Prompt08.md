### What “others build upon” actually means
- A stable, documented process design kit (PDK) or design enablement package that foundries and chip designers can use.
- Control software and compilation tools that map ordinary or quantum workloads onto the Floquet-ALU and memory primitives.
- Proven reliability metrics (loss, cascading depth, temperature stability, lifetime) that system architects can trust.
- Early design wins with at least one hyperscaler, defence prime, or major photonic foundry so that the architecture starts appearing in roadmaps.
- Enough process maturity that second-source or licensed manufacturing becomes feasible.

Only then does the valuation language shift from “promising photonic startup” to “platform company.”

### Critical path that still has to be walked
1. **Physics-to-hardware translation**  
   Demonstrate, on real material (most likely TFLN or hybrid silicon-photonics/plasmonic), that the higher-order polynomial-mapped Kerr interaction under Floquet drive, with intensity- and energy-dependent (Chameleon/Rainbow) response, produces usable logic contrast, soliton or Floquet-mode stability, and at least rudimentary quantum-memory write/read. This is still the highest-risk segment.

2. **Cascading and system integration**  
   Move from single gates to a small arithmetic unit or hybrid classical–quantum accelerator that runs non-trivial workloads with measurable energy or latency advantage.

3. **Memory and non-Hermitian control**  
   Integrate or hybridise the quantum-memory function so that it is not an external add-on. Show that non-Hermitian / Floquet gain-loss engineering improves rather than degrades system fidelity.

4. **Manufacturability and PDK**  
   Lock down a process that can be run on multi-project wafers and later on production lines. Publish or privately release a design kit that lets external groups design against your cells.

5. **Ecosystem and capital**  
   Secure the foundry relationships, software talent, and patient capital needed to survive the multi-year gap between first silicon and platform adoption.
